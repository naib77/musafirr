# Major Disruptive Events Policy

**Musaafir** · Last updated 30 August 2026

Sometimes an event bigger than any one booking — a cyclone, a curfew, an
epidemic — makes stays in an area impossible or unsafe. This policy says what
happens then. It overrides the normal cancellation tiers in our
[Refund & Cancellation Policy](refund-policy.md), which continue to apply to
everything this policy does not cover.

**The short version.** If a large-scale event prevents or legally prohibits
your stay, you can cancel for a **full refund** no matter how close to
check-in you are, and the host can cancel without any penalty — but the host
is not paid for the cancelled dates either.

## 1. When this policy applies

We activate this policy for a specific area and period when an event there:

- began **after** the booking was made,
- affects the **place of the stay** (not just your own plans), and
- makes completing the stay impossible, unlawful, or genuinely unsafe.

Events that qualify:

- **Public health emergencies.** Government-declared epidemics and public
  health emergencies affecting the area. Endemic seasonal illness (for
  example, ordinary seasonal dengue levels) does not qualify on its own.
- **Government restrictions on movement.** A curfew, a Section 144 or
  equivalent prohibitory order covering the stay's area, a mandatory
  evacuation order, or an official closure of the area. Advice to "avoid
  unnecessary travel" is not an order and does not qualify.
- **Civil unrest and armed conflict.** Riots, widespread political violence,
  or armed conflict in the area. A hartal qualifies only when it comes with a
  curfew or genuine, area-wide violence — a peaceful strike day that makes
  travel inconvenient does not.
- **Natural disasters.** A cyclone with a government evacuation or warning
  signal for the area, an earthquake, or flooding **beyond what is normal for
  that place and season**. Bangladesh's ordinary monsoon is foreseeable:
  routine seasonal rain, and routine seasonal water levels in areas that flood
  every year, do not qualify. A flood that submerges areas that do not
  normally flood does.
- **Prolonged loss of essential utilities.** Electricity, water, or gas out
  across most of the area for a prolonged period — not a routine load-shedding
  schedule, and not an outage in one building (that is a
  [booking issue](refund-policy.md), and the normal refund rules for it are
  usually better for you anyway).

## 2. What is not covered

These do not qualify, however genuinely disruptive they are to you:

- Anything affecting **you** rather than the stay's area — your own illness or
  injury, a family emergency, a court date.
- **The reason for your trip falling through.** An exam that is postponed, a
  medical appointment that is moved, an event that is cancelled. The stay
  itself was still available; the normal cancellation tiers in the
  [Refund & Cancellation Policy](refund-policy.md) apply.
- **Transport problems** — flight or launch cancellations, road closures,
  transport strikes — unless caused by an event that itself qualifies above.
- **Foreseeable weather** for the place and season, as described above.
- Events that ended, or were already known, **before you booked**. If you
  booked into a declared disaster area, you booked knowing.

## 3. What you get

When this policy applies to your booking:

- **Guests** may cancel and receive a **100% refund** of whatever they paid,
  regardless of timing. Refunds are paid the way the
  [Refund & Cancellation Policy](refund-policy.md) §7 describes.
- **Hosts** may cancel without any of the penalties that normally follow a
  host cancellation — no standing damage, no enforcement. The host receives no
  payout for the cancelled dates, and if a payout was already made, we recover
  the refunded amount from future payouts.
- Where only part of a stay is affected, the unaffected nights already spent
  are paid for and the remainder is refunded.

## 4. How it works in practice

We — not the guest or the host individually — decide that this policy is
active for an area and a period, based on government declarations, disaster
warnings, and what is actually verifiable on the ground. When we activate it
we say so to everyone with an affected booking, in the app.

If your booking is affected and you have not heard from us, contact
**support@musaafir.app** with your booking reference and what is happening.
One person's claim that an event qualifies is reviewed by a person, not
auto-approved.

## 5. Changes

We may change this policy; the date at the top changes when we do. A claim is
handled under the policy in force when the booking was made.
