# Guidelines for Law Enforcement

**Musaafir** · Last updated 30 August 2026

These guidelines explain how law enforcement agencies can request user data
from Musaafir, operated by iObytes, Dhaka, Bangladesh, and what we do when a
request arrives. Our users are told, in our
[Privacy Policy](privacy-policy.md), that we disclose data where legally
required or where necessary to investigate fraud, abuse, or a threat to
someone's safety. This page is how that happens in practice.

## 1. How to send a request

Email **support@musaafir.app** with the subject **"Law enforcement request"**.
A valid request must:

- come from an official government email address, or attach a signed request
  on the agency's letterhead;
- identify the requesting officer — name, designation, unit, and a callback
  contact we can independently verify;
- cite the **legal authority** for the request (the applicable law, court
  order, or warrant, attached where one exists);
- identify the account concerned. Our account identifier is the **mobile
  number** the user signs in with; a name alone is usually not enough to
  locate an account reliably;
- state **specifically what data is sought and for what period**, and how it
  relates to the investigation.

We respond to requests about our users; we cannot answer for other companies'
services. We review every request for validity and scope, we may ask for
narrowing or for the underlying legal process, and we may decline requests
that are overbroad or lack a lawful basis.

## 2. What we actually hold

Musaafir is a stays marketplace, and we hold what our
[Privacy Policy](privacy-policy.md) says we hold — no more. In law-enforcement
terms:

- **Account data** — mobile number, name, optional email, profile photo,
  account creation date.
- **Host verification data** — national ID number and images, selfie,
  proof-of-address document, for verified hosts only.
- **Listings** — property address, coordinates, photos, pricing.
- **Booking records** — who stayed where, dates, price, status, timestamps.
- **Payment and payout records** — gateway transaction references, amounts,
  payout account details (wallet or bank), disbursement records.
- **Messages** — in-app chat between a guest and a host.

Things we are regularly asked for that we **do not have**:

- **Login one-time codes** — stored only as hashes and expired within minutes.
  We cannot produce or reproduce a code, past or future.
- **Continuous or background location** — never collected. Location is used
  transiently for search; a user's whereabouts are not tracked.
- **Voice recordings** — voice-search audio never reaches our servers.
- **Browsing or ad-tracking history** — we have no advertising or
  cross-app tracking.

## 3. Emergency disclosure

Where there is a genuine emergency — an imminent risk of death or serious
physical harm to someone — email the same address with
**"EMERGENCY"** first in the subject line, describing the emergency, the
person at risk, and the specific data that would help. We prioritise these,
verify the requester by callback, and may disclose the minimum data necessary
to address the emergency before formal process completes, with the formal
process to follow.

## 4. Preservation

On a valid written request we will preserve a snapshot of specified account
data for **90 days** (extendable once on request) while legal process for its
disclosure is obtained. Preservation is not disclosure: nothing is handed over
until a valid request for the data itself arrives.

## 5. Telling the user

Our default is transparency: where the law does not prohibit it and doing so
would not endanger anyone or compromise an active investigation, we tell a
user when their data has been requested. If a request must remain
confidential, say so and cite the legal basis for the confidentiality.
